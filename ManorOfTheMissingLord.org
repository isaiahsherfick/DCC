#+title: Manor of the Missing Lord == A DCC Adventure for 4-6 level 1-2 adventurers
30 Aug 2026
* Judge Introduction
    The setting in which this adventure takes place is an ordinary instance
of feudal society. Peasants toil about their daily lives, farming simple crops,
raising livestock, and producing basic textiles. Over these peasants presides a
lord. This lord is an arrogant prick who produces nothing useful. He frequently
visits his peasantry demanding arbitrary taxes and dues with little rhyme or
reason. He is not popular among the peasantry, but this is the way of things. It
is easiest to simply grumble "yes, milord," and produce whatever he demands.
Sometimes it is coin. Sometimes it is the ripest crops of the harvest or the
choicest cuts of meat. Sometimes it is the eldest son for a territory dispute
with a neighboring lord. Again, such is the way of things.  Refusing to comply
is a death sentence at best.

   This has been the way of things for as long as anyone nearby can remember. At
least, it was -- before this latest harvest. As the leaves of autumn settle beneath
the chilling winds of change that foreshadow another harsh winter, it marks the
hundredth day of complete silence from the lord -- completely unheard of. Rumors
have begun to spread among the commonfolk of the region; what has happened to
the lord? Has he fallen victim to some kind of illness? Has he journeyed away to
some distant land? And, most importantly, what's stopping one of us from making
the trek to his lavish manor and stuffing their pockets with his treasure and
riches? As more and more time passes without anyone receiving so much as a
collection letter from the lord, this idea becomes more and more tantalizing in
the peoples' minds.

    Unbeknownst to the players, the lord of the land is in fact dead. His greed
and wealth attracted a Treasure Devourer to his manor about a year ago, a
magical monstrosity that feeds on treasure. The Treasure Devourer begins its
lifecycle as a single microscopic spore, then plants itself like a fungus in a
treasury. If noticed early, Treasure Devourers can be taken care of with
relative ease. Left unchecked to grow and feed on a vast amount of wealth and
treasure, they can grow to immense size and overtake entire rooms, buildings, or
settlements in extreme cases. The larger they get, the more powerful their
magical energy. This energy attracts other nearby monsters and beings sensitive
to a magical presence.

** Area 0: Stable
*** Read-aloud
    You enter into a stable that is fairly ordinary, although a bit large for a
private residence. Three horse-sized stalls line the walls. Three saddles and
other miscellaneous equestrian supplies hang from the walls, and a heavy musk of
hay and dirt hangs in the air. One of the three stalls is currently occupied by
a brown adult horse.
*** Judge Notes
- Horse is actually a disguised appendage of the Treasure Devourer waiting to
  ambush creatures that get too close
- Horse is "breathing" (chest is rising and falling) but air is not passing (Intelligence DC15)
- Horse's tail is sitting still in a pile of hay. The tail is actually a long,
  veiny appendage that connects to the TD all the way back in the treasury. (Intelligence DC15)
- The horse will make a surprise bite attack on the first player to enter the
  still and get close to the horse. d24 + 2 to hit. The Treasure
  Devourer will then pull the character into the ground and straight to its
  stomach. If the horse misses, it will continue to make bite attacks until it
  either: takes 20 damage, has its tail severed by a Mighty Deed, or
  successfully bites a character and drags them away.
- A bitten character loses consciousness before reaching the stomach of the
  devourer. If asked whether the taken character is dead, simply state that if
  the corpse can be located, the character can be turned over.
- If any of the characters have a magical item or particularly valuable piece of
  treasure on their person, the horse will prioritize that character or specific
  piece of gear if it makes sense.
- For example, if one player has a magical sword on their belt, the horse will
  bite the sword instead of the character. Have the PC make a Luck check -- on
  fail, they are dragged down with the sword. On success, it is ripped from
  their belt and they are spared.
- If attacked from a distance, the horse immediately retreats.
- Whether it successfully ambushes a character or retreats, be sure to
  viscerally describe the way the horse's bones break upwards and crunch as it
  is forced through the small tunnel that was dug by the appendage of the
  Treasure Devourer.
*** Lootables
- 100ft rope on the wall
- Three saddles (use book value)
*** XP Rewards
- If the false horse is discovered before an ambush is triggered and either attacked or avoided, award 3 XP to all players.
- If a player is ambushed by surprise, award survivors 1 XP
** Area 0.5: Manor Exterior / Main Entrance
*** Read-aloud
    Compared to the thatched-roof cottages and small huts that fill the nearby
villages and towns, the manor at the top of this hill may as well be a palace.
The exterior has been painted white, and the trail on which you stand leads to a
large set of engraved double doors at the center of the southern face.
*** Key features
- Double doors are unlocked and untrapped
- If characters circumnavigate the building, the western wall contains two
  windows on the second story (15ft up), one of which is halfway open. This
  window leads to Area 5, the bedroom.
** Lobby
*** Read-aloud
    The double doors reveal a large lobby with a 25 foot ceiling. The floor is
black-and-white checkered stone. On the first floor, two closed wooden doors sit
along the west wall, and three closed wooden doors sit along the east wall. In
the middle of the room, a curved set of symmetrical wooden stairs meet at the
first floor, leading to a second floor wooden railing along the northern side of
the room.
*** Key features
- Door C (the middle door on the eastern wall) is a mimic door appendage of the
  Treasure Devourer. A character who opens the door is surprised by a long,
  slimy black tongue which coils around the character on a successful DC24+4
  attack roll. The character may make a DC 17 strength check to resist being
  pulled down into the barbed fleshy throat.  The character loses consciousness
  after one round in the Treasure Devourer's stomach. Describe the interior of
  the stomach as moist, cramped, and completely dark. If a character attempts to
  cut their way out of the stomach, it is as though their blade is striking
  metal. A character who uses their one turn to create any kind of fire in the
  Treasure Devourer's stomach will cause it to regurgitate its stomach contents
  back into the lobby. If this is the first area visited since a character was
  swallowed in the stable, that character's roll-over check automatically
  succeeds. In addition to the regurgitated characters, 100 half-melted gold
  pieces, 5000 half-melted silver pieces, and 25,000 half-melted copper pieces
  float in a puddle of steaming almost-black green sludge that drifts from the
  door into the lobby. The TD retreats its door-mouth back into the Treasury if
  this happens.  If asked whether the taken character is dead, simply state that
  if the corpse can be located, the character can be turned over.
- A large chandlier worth 100GP hangs from the top of the center of the room.
*** XP Rewards
- Reward all characters who were not swallowed by the door 1 XP
- If the treasurer devourer is forced to regurgitate, reward all characters 3 XP.

** STRT "Library"/Kitchen+Dining Room
*** Read-aloud
**** Entering from door A (Description of dining room)
Three floor-to-ceiling bookshelves stand flush against one another on the
western wall of this room filled with colorful volumes. A long 30 foot dining
table surrounded by ten stools sits in the center of the room.
**** Entering from door E (Description of kitchen)
Five floor-to-ceiling bookshelves line the walls of this room. The north wall
contains an open door.
*** Key features
- Bookshelves are false and filled with Vampire Books.
- If a player says they "look around" or want to "investigate" the room, ask
  them which room (north or south).
- There is a trapdoor in the southeastern corner underneath one of the
  bookshelves which can be discovered with a DC 5 intelligence check by a PC
  investigating the kitchen. The PCs must move the bookshelf out of the way to
  access the trapdoor. No check is required to move the bookshelf, but doing so
  activates all of the Vampire Books if they haven't already been defeated.
- From either room, a DC 12 intelligence check will give the PC a strong feeling
  that this room is structured like a kitchen and dining room, not a library.
- Dwarves are entitled to a DC 5 check to know that the bookshelves are not part
  of the original room.
- Elves are entitled to the DC 5 intelligence check to discover the trapdoor
  upon entering the kitchen.
- There is a dumb waiter (little elevator for sending food up to the lord's
  bedroom) in the northern section of the easternmost dining room wall which can
  be discovered with a DC 5 intelligence check by a PC investigating the dining
  room. Only a halfling can fit in the dumb waiter to scout out the bedroom.

** STRT Wine Cellar
*** Read-aloud
The trapdoor reveals a dusty staircase that descends into a large basement filled floor-to-ceiling with wine racks.
*** Key features
- Each PC that descends the stairs must make a Luck check. All characters who
  fail are bitten by a small spider and must make a DC 10 Fortitude saving
  throw. on a fail, the PC falls paralyzed after a single dungeon turn and
  remains paralyzed until >= 1 HP is restored by any means. On a success, the PC
  becomes dizzy and sluggish after 1 dungeon turn: -1d on all rolls until >=1 HP
  is restored by any means.
- The giant spider emerges and begins dragging paralyzed party members into a
  burrow it's dug in the far corner of the room
- Servants and cooks are hollowed out in silk cocoons in the burrow.
- Exquisite spices worth 50 GP can be found in the apron of the head chef if his
  coccoon is opened by the players.
- Players can make off with as many bottles of fine wine as they can carry.
*** XP Rewards
- Award 2 XP for defeating the giant spider and 1 XP for discovering the
  exquisite spices.
